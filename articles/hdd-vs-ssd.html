HDD vs SSD: Which Is Better for Your PC?

If your computer takes ages to start or open apps, the storage drive could be part of the problem. This guide explains HDDs and SSDs in plain language so you can make a practical upgrade decision.

Know Your PC · Beginner-friendly guide

What is a storage drive?

A storage drive keeps your files even when your computer is switched off. Windows, applications, photos, videos, games and documents are all stored on a drive.

What is an HDD?

An HDD (hard disk drive) stores data on spinning magnetic disks. It can offer a lot of space for a relatively low price, which makes it useful for large photo, video and document collections. Because it has moving parts, it is generally slower and more sensitive to physical knocks while operating.

What is an SSD?

An SSD (solid-state drive) stores data on flash memory and has no spinning disks. It can load Windows and applications much faster than a typical HDD, works silently and handles everyday movement better. SSDs often cost more per gigabyte, although prices vary by capacity, type and retailer.

Will an SSD make an old PC faster?

It can make a noticeable difference if the computer currently uses an HDD, especially when starting Windows, opening programs and loading files. It will not automatically fix every problem: an older processor, low memory, overheating, malware or a failing drive can also affect performance.

Before buying: Check whether your computer supports a 2.5-inch SATA SSD, an M.2 SATA drive or an M.2 NVMe drive. These are not interchangeable in every computer. Check the model's manual or ask a trusted technician before purchasing.

How to choose

  • For a faster everyday computer: An SSD is usually the best first storage upgrade if your PC supports it.
  • For lots of files on a tight budget: An HDD can still be useful as secondary storage.
  • For a laptop: Confirm the physical size, connection type and whether the drive can be replaced.
  • For important files: Keep a separate backup. Any drive can fail, whether it is an HDD or SSD.

Back up before upgrading

Before replacing or cloning a drive, back up your important documents, photos and other files to a separate drive or trusted cloud service. If you are not comfortable opening your computer or reinstalling Windows, get help from a technician.

Final thoughts

For most people who want a more responsive PC, an SSD is the better choice for the main Windows drive. An HDD can still be useful for affordable extra storage. Always check compatibility and back up your data before making changes.
