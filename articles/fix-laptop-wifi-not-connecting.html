How to Fix a Laptop That Won't Connect to Wi-Fi (Windows 10 & 11)

By Know Your PC | Beginner Guide | 8–10 min read

Is your laptop refusing to connect to Wi-Fi? Maybe your phone connects perfectly, but your Windows laptop keeps showing "Can't connect to this network", "No Internet", or no available Wi-Fi networks at all.

The good news is that many Wi-Fi problems can be fixed without paying for repairs or buying new equipment.

In this beginner-friendly guide from Know Your PC, we'll walk through practical ways to troubleshoot Wi-Fi problems on Windows 10 and Windows 11.

Quick tip: Start with the easiest solutions first. You don't need advanced computer skills to follow this guide.

1. Identify the Wi-Fi Problem

Before changing settings, check what is actually happening.

  • Wi-Fi network is missing: Your laptop cannot see the wireless network.
  • Can't connect to this network: The laptop detects Wi-Fi but fails to connect.
  • Connected, no internet: The laptop joins the network, but websites do not load.
  • Wi-Fi option is missing: Windows may not be detecting the wireless adapter.

Try connecting another device, such as your smartphone, to the same Wi-Fi network.

If several devices cannot access the internet, the issue may be with your router, fibre connection, mobile router, or internet provider.

If only your laptop is affected, continue with the next steps.

2. Restart Your Laptop and Wi-Fi Router

Restarting can clear temporary connection problems.

Restart your laptop

  1. Save your open work.
  2. Open the Start menu.
  3. Select Power.
  4. Choose Restart.
  5. Try connecting to Wi-Fi again.

Restart your router

  1. Switch off your Wi-Fi router.
  2. Wait about 30 seconds.
  3. Switch it back on.
  4. Wait for the connection lights to stabilise.
  5. Try connecting again.
Important: Do not press the small factory-reset button on your router. That may erase its configuration and require your internet provider to set it up again.

3. Check Your Laptop's Wi-Fi Settings

Wi-Fi might be disabled accidentally, or Airplane mode might be enabled.

On Windows 11

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Select Network & internet.
  3. Make sure Wi-Fi is turned on.
  4. Make sure Airplane mode is turned off.
  5. Select your network and try connecting.

On Windows 10

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Select Network & Internet.
  3. Choose Wi-Fi.
  4. Turn Wi-Fi on.
  5. Check that Airplane mode is off.

Some laptops also have a wireless function key or physical Wi-Fi switch. Check your laptop's keyboard and user manual if the Wi-Fi option is unavailable.

4. Forget the Wi-Fi Network and Reconnect

Windows may have saved an outdated network configuration or password.

Windows 11

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Go to Network & internet.
  3. Select Wi-Fi.
  4. Open Manage known networks.
  5. Find your Wi-Fi network and select Forget.
  6. Connect again using the correct password.

Windows 10

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Go to Network & Internet.
  3. Select Wi-Fi.
  4. Choose Manage known networks.
  5. Select your network and click Forget.
  6. Reconnect using the Wi-Fi password.
Remember: You will need your Wi-Fi password to reconnect. Make sure you know it before forgetting the network.

5. Run Windows Network Diagnostics

Windows includes built-in tools that can help identify connection problems.

Windows 11

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Select System.
  3. Open Troubleshoot.
  4. Select Other troubleshooters.
  5. Run the available network or internet troubleshooter, if shown.

On newer Windows 11 versions, Windows may direct you to network diagnostics through the Get Help app instead.

Windows 10

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Select Update & Security.
  3. Open Troubleshoot.
  4. Select Additional troubleshooters.
  5. Run Internet Connections.

Follow the instructions displayed on your screen, then test your connection.

6. Check Your Wi-Fi Adapter Driver

A missing, disabled, or malfunctioning Wi-Fi driver can stop your laptop from connecting.

  1. Right-click the Start button.
  2. Select Device Manager.
  3. Expand Network adapters.
  4. Look for an adapter with words such as Wireless, Wi-Fi, WLAN, or 802.11.
  5. If the adapter is disabled, right-click it and select Enable device.
  6. If a warning symbol appears, check the device's Properties for an error message.

If you need a new driver, download it from your laptop manufacturer's official support website using the exact laptop model.

If your laptop has no internet connection, you may need another device to download the driver and transfer it using a USB drive.

Safety warning: Avoid random driver-download websites and software claiming to fix every driver problem automatically.

7. Reset Windows Network Settings

If the previous steps fail, a network reset may help restore the default network configuration.

Windows 11

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Select Network & internet.
  3. Open Advanced network settings.
  4. Select Network reset.
  5. Choose Reset now.
  6. Restart when prompted.

Windows 10

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Select Network & Internet.
  3. Open Status.
  4. Select Network reset.
  5. Choose Reset now.
Before resetting: A network reset removes and reinstalls network adapters and resets their settings. You may need to reconnect to Wi-Fi and reconfigure VPN software or other custom network settings.

8. What If Your Laptop Still Won't Connect?

If none of these methods work, try the following checks:

  • Test your laptop with another trusted Wi-Fi network.
  • Check whether your router's internet connection is working.
  • Check your internet provider's service status if multiple devices are affected.
  • Check your laptop manufacturer's support website for known wireless issues.
  • Consider professional diagnosis if the wireless adapter is not detected at all.

If your laptop is also performing slowly, read our guide explaining why a PC becomes slow .

You can also check your computer specifications to help identify the correct laptop model and hardware.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my phone connect to Wi-Fi but my laptop doesn't?

Your laptop may have incorrect network settings, an outdated or malfunctioning Wi-Fi driver, or a problem with its wireless adapter. Start by restarting the laptop and forgetting the saved Wi-Fi network.

Why does my laptop say connected but no internet?

Your laptop may be connected to the router without having a working internet connection. Test another device and restart the router. If all devices are affected, contact your internet provider.

Can a laptop's Wi-Fi adapter stop working?

Yes. Wireless adapters can develop hardware faults. However, check software settings, drivers, and network configuration before assuming the adapter is broken.

Will resetting my network delete personal files?

A Windows network reset does not normally delete documents, pictures, or other personal files. It does reset network-related settings, which may require reconfiguration.

Final Thoughts

A laptop that won't connect to Wi-Fi can be frustrating, especially when you need internet access for school, work, or everyday tasks.

Fortunately, many connection problems can be solved by restarting your devices, checking wireless settings, reconnecting to your network, or fixing a driver issue.

Work through the solutions in order and avoid changing advanced settings unless you understand what they do.
