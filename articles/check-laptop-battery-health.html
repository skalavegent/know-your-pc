How to Check Laptop Battery Health in Windows

Does your laptop battery drain quickly, shut down unexpectedly, or last much less time than it used to? Windows includes a built-in battery report that can help you check the battery's reported capacity without installing extra software.

Reading time: 5 minutes

Before you start

This guide is for Windows laptops. The battery report is available on many Windows 10 and Windows 11 devices, but the details shown can vary by manufacturer and battery hardware. You normally do not need to download an app or pay for a diagnostic tool.

If your laptop battery or case is visibly swollen, the trackpad or keyboard is lifting, or the device smells unusual or becomes dangerously hot, stop using and charging it. Do not press, puncture, or attempt to remove a swollen battery yourself. Arrange professional service.

Step 1: Generate a battery report

  1. Save your work and open the Windows Start menu.
  2. Type Command Prompt in the search box.
  3. Open Command Prompt. Standard access is usually enough; if Windows reports that permission is required, try opening it as an administrator.
  4. Type this command and press Enter:
powercfg /batteryreport

Windows should display a message showing where it saved the report. The default location is often inside your user folder, with a filename such as battery-report.html. The exact path may differ.

  1. Open File Explorer and navigate to the path shown in Command Prompt.
  2. Open battery-report.html in your browser.

If the command is not recognised or the report cannot be created, check that you are using Windows and try Command Prompt again. Some devices or managed work computers may restrict access to certain diagnostics.

Step 2: Understand the important numbers

Look for the Installed batteries section. Two values are particularly useful:

  • Design Capacity: the battery's reported capacity when it was new.
  • Full Charge Capacity: the amount of energy the battery can currently hold according to Windows' report.

For example, imagine the report shows a design capacity of 50,000 mWh and a full charge capacity of 35,000 mWh. The reported capacity is approximately 70% of the original design capacity.

You can estimate it with this formula:

(Full Charge Capacity ÷ Design Capacity) × 100

Using the example above: (35,000 ÷ 50,000) × 100 = 70%.

This is an estimate based on the values reported by the battery system, not a perfect laboratory measurement. Capacity readings can vary, and battery health alone does not explain every case of short runtime.

What does the percentage mean?

  • Closer to 100%: reported capacity is closer to the original design capacity.
  • Noticeably lower than when new: the battery has lost some of its original capacity, which is normal as rechargeable batteries age.
  • Very low or changing unexpectedly: consider checking the laptop manufacturer's diagnostics or asking a qualified technician to assess the battery.

There is no single percentage that proves every laptop battery must be replaced. Consider actual runtime, unexpected shutdowns, battery warnings, the laptop manufacturer's guidance, and whether the battery is physically safe.

Check recent battery usage

The report may also include recent usage, usage history, and battery capacity history. These sections can help you spot changes over time, but their availability and detail depend on the device and how long Windows has collected data.

Step 3: Improve everyday battery life

If the battery is safe and the laptop works normally, these changes may help it last longer between charges. They do not restore capacity that the battery has already lost.

  1. Reduce screen brightness. Use a comfortable brightness level rather than maximum brightness all day.
  2. Enable Battery Saver or Energy Saver. Find the power options in Windows Settings; the exact name and location can differ by Windows version.
  3. Close apps you do not need. Check Task Manager for apps using unusually high CPU or memory resources.
  4. Review startup apps. Disable unnecessary startup apps using Windows settings or Task Manager. Do not disable unfamiliar system processes at random.
  5. Install trusted Windows and manufacturer updates. Updates can resolve some power-management problems. Use Windows Update or your laptop manufacturer's official support site.
  6. Check power mode. A power-saving mode may extend runtime, while a performance mode can use more energy.

If the laptop drains rapidly even while idle, becomes unusually hot, or loses charge while shut down, investigate further rather than assuming the battery alone is responsible.

When should you consider replacing the battery?

Consider a professional assessment if the battery no longer lasts long enough for your needs, Windows shows a battery-service warning, the laptop shuts down unexpectedly despite showing charge, or the reported capacity has fallen significantly.

Before buying a replacement, confirm your exact laptop model and the compatible battery part number. Use the manufacturer's support information or a reputable repairer. Avoid choosing a battery based on appearance alone, and do not open a laptop or handle an internal battery unless you have the appropriate skills and instructions.

If you are considering a second-hand laptop, check the battery report alongside the device's overall condition, performance, and charging behaviour. A battery report is useful evidence, but it is not a guarantee of the laptop's condition.

Final thoughts

Windows' battery report is a free first step for understanding your laptop's battery capacity. Compare design capacity with full charge capacity, consider the laptop's real-world runtime, and get professional help if the battery looks damaged or behaves unsafely.
